How much does a website cost?

The same brief produces quotes of £800 and £25,000. Both are serious, and they describe entirely different things. This page sets out the five factors behind that gap, so you can compare quotes rather than line up numbers.

Request a quote What drives it

Rough ranges

Typical UK and Irish market bands, before recurring costs.

01

Website builder, done yourself — £0 to £400

A ready-made template, your copy, your time. Useful for testing whether an idea has legs. Not useful when the site has to produce enquiries: it looks like a thousand others and is effectively invisible in search.

02

Small business site — £1,500 to £4,000

Five to ten pages, adapted design, written copy, SEO groundwork, enquiry form. For most trades and service businesses, this is where the spend starts paying for itself.

03

Full company website — £4,000 to £12,000

More pages, multiple languages, product catalogue, customer area, connections to systems you already run. Most B2B projects land in this band.

04

E-commerce — from £6,000

Product data, variants, payment, shipping, stock integration. The price depends almost entirely on how many products and connections are involved, and very little on the design.

The five factors

What actually sets the price

First, page count — though not the way people assume. The expensive page is not the tenth, it is the first, because that is where the whole design system gets built. Second, the copy. Supplying it yourself lowers a quote noticeably; having it written costs money and usually produces a site that is found more easily, because the text is written towards phrases people actually search.

Third, languages. A second language version does not double the price; it adds roughly a third, provided it is planned from the start. Added afterwards it costs considerably more, because the structure has to be rebuilt around it.

Fourth, integrations: stock system, CRM, booking platform, payment provider. This is the factor that spreads quotes furthest apart, because it rarely appears in the original brief and only surfaces in conversation. Fifth, the SEO groundwork — which some suppliers price as an extra when it is simply part of the job. A site that cannot be found is not finished.

  • Page count. The first page costs the most; every one after it costs considerably less.
  • Copy. Supplying it lowers the quote. Having it written costs more and usually improves visibility.
  • Languages. Around a third more when planned upfront, considerably more when added later.
  • Integrations. The widest-varying factor, and the one almost always missing from the brief.
What comes after

The recurring costs quotes rarely mention

Domain and hosting run to roughly £100 to £300 a year for an ordinary business site. On top sit updates and backups, either as a maintenance retainer or as your own time. Ignoring both leaves you, two years on, with a site carrying known security holes — which is not a theoretical risk.

The cost that actually matters, though, appears in no website quote at all: the budget for being found. A website is not a shop on a busy street; nobody walks past until someone makes them. Spending £5,000 on the site and nothing on visibility produces a good site nobody sees.

Comparing quotes

Five questions
that expose the difference.

Asked of every supplier, they make quotes comparable that did not look comparable at all.

  1. Are the domain, hosting and code in my name?If not, the low price is a deposit on a long dependency.
  2. Is the copy included?The single most common reason two quotes sit so far apart.
  3. What does a change cost after launch?Ask for a specific example and a number, not an hourly rate.
  4. Is the SEO groundwork included?Structure, tags, structured data, sitemap, Search Console. If it is an extra, the site is not being handed over finished.
  5. What happens if we move to another supplier?The answer tells you more about the supplier than the price does.
Frequently asked

The answers that actually matter.

Why does the same website cost three times more elsewhere?

Usually three things: whether copy is included, whether the design is bespoke or a bought template, and whether systems are being integrated. Location matters too — a London agency carries a very different cost base from a supplier in Northern Italy for identical work.

Is an expensive website automatically better?

No, but a very cheap one is almost always worse. Below about £800 there is simply no time left for original copy, a considered structure and the SEO groundwork: the price is reached with a ready-made template and your own labour.

What does an e-commerce site cost?

From around £6,000 for a shop with real product pages, payment, shipping and a stock connection. Price follows the number of products and variants and the integrations; design is the smallest line item.

Are website builders worth it?

For testing whether an idea works, yes. As soon as the site has to produce enquiries seriously, you meet three limits: an interchangeable look, mediocre load times and restricted SEO control. Then there is the monthly fee, which never ends.

How do I spot a quote that is not serious?

Three signs: a number given without a single question about your business, a guaranteed first place on Google, and hosting that is not in your name. Any one of them is enough of a warning on its own.
